Septoria Leaf Spot

Medium Risk

🔍 Symptoms

Small, circular spots (3–5 mm) with dark brown borders and light grey or tan centers. Tiny black dots (pycnidia) visible in the center. Yellowing and wilting of affected leaves.

🦠 Cause

Caused by the fungus Septoria lycopersici. Spreads through water splashing from infected soil, high humidity, and temperatures of 20–25°C.

💊 Treatment

  • Apply copper-based or chlorothalonil fungicides
  • Remove and destroy infected leaves
  • Avoid overhead watering
  • Rotate crops annually

Early Blight

High Risk

🔍 Symptoms

Dark brown to black lesions with distinctive concentric rings forming a "target" pattern. Yellowing tissue around lesions. Appears on older leaves first, moves upward.

🦠 Cause

Caused by Alternaria solani fungus. Favors warm temperatures (24–29°C) with high humidity. Spread by wind, rain splash, and infected plant debris.

💊 Treatment

  • Apply fungicides (mancozeb, chlorothalonil)
  • Remove diseased foliage promptly
  • Ensure proper plant spacing for air circulation
  • Use disease-resistant tomato varieties
📅 Season: Warm & Wet 🎯 Model Accuracy: 95%
🌑

Late Blight

High Risk

🔍 Symptoms

Irregular, water-soaked lesions that turn brown/black. White cottony growth on leaf undersides. Rapid plant collapse in wet weather. Also infects stems and fruits.

🦠 Cause

Caused by Phytophthora infestans — an oomycete (water mold). Thrives in cool (10–20°C), moist conditions. Can devastate crops within days.

💊 Treatment

  • Apply metalaxyl or cymoxanil fungicides immediately
  • Remove all affected plant material
  • Avoid planting near potatoes
  • Use certified disease-free seeds
📅 Season: Cool & Moist 🎯 Model Accuracy: 93%
🌀

Tomato Mosaic Virus

Medium Risk

🔍 Symptoms

Mottled yellow-green pattern on leaves (mosaic pattern). Leaf curling, distortion, and stunted growth. Fruits may show blotchy ripening and reduced yield.

🦠 Cause

Caused by Tomato mosaic virus (ToMV). Spreads through contact (hands, tools), infected seeds, and grafting. Highly persistent in soil and plant debris.

💊 Treatment

  • No chemical cure available — prevention is key
  • Use virus-free certified seeds
  • Disinfect tools with bleach solution
  • Remove and destroy infected plants
📅 Season: Year-round 🎯 Model Accuracy: 91%
🌿

Yellow Leaf Curl Virus

High Risk

🔍 Symptoms

Severe upward curling and yellowing of leaves. Stunted plant growth, flower drop, and very low fruit production. New growth appears crumpled and distorted.

🦠 Cause

Caused by Tomato yellow leaf curl virus (TYLCV). Transmitted exclusively by the whitefly (Bemisia tabaci). Major problem in tropical and subtropical regions.

💊 Treatment

  • Control whitefly populations with insecticides
  • Use reflective mulch to repel whiteflies
  • Plant resistant tomato varieties
  • Remove infected plants immediately
📅 Season: Hot & Dry 🎯 Model Accuracy: 89%
✅

Healthy Plant

No Risk

🔍 Signs of Health

Deep green, uniform leaf color. No spots, lesions, or discoloration. Firm, upright stems. Normal leaf shape without curling or distortion.

🌱 Maintenance Tips

Regular watering, proper drainage, balanced NPK fertilization, adequate sunlight (6–8 hours), and weekly scouting for early disease signs.

💊 Prevention

  • Use disease-resistant seed varieties
  • Practice crop rotation every season
  • Maintain proper plant spacing
  • Scan leaves regularly with CropGuard AI
📅 Year-round care 🎯 Model Accuracy: 99%
